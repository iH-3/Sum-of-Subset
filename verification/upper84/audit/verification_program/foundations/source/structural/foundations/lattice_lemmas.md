# Primitive effective-hole extension and short-diameter sumset bound

## Primitive effective-hole extension

After the initial integralization, scale the entire configuration by1/gcd(Q). All cardinalities and defects are unchanged; Q becomes a primitive integer set, while P is initially only rational/real.

The primitive version of the existing effective-hole lemma is valid here. In the original proof the condition h<|FS|−1 is used only to infer primitivity. Given primitivity directly, positive holes exclude an AP support, and the remaining tail, counted-hole, off-scale, and endpoint-bridge arguments are unchanged. Thus for explicit admissible parameters beta,C_h, the condition h_Q>2beta implies mu_Q≥b+C_h+1 without requiring a separate upper bound on h_Q.


## Short-diameter theorem

For finite integer sets U,V containing 0 and their respective hull endpoints, let u=|U|, v=|V| and h_P,h_Q be the two hull-hole counts.

Stanchescu Theorem4 applies when its stated diameter hypothesis holds. Its short-diameter hypothesis is max(diam U,diam V)≤u+v−2−delta, with delta1 for equal diameters and0 otherwise; it has no additional gcd hypothesis. It yields

|U+V|≥u+v−1+max(h_P,h_Q).

If kappa=|U+V|−u−v+1, the conclusion is h_P,h_Q≤kappa. Consequently z=h_P+h_Q−kappa satisfies 0≤z≤min(h_P,h_Q).

Reference: Y. Stanchescu, On addition of two distinct sets of integers, Acta Arithmetica 75 (1996), 191–194, Theorem 4 and Corollary 2. https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-75-2-191-194
