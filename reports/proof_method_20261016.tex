\begin{abstract}
We simplify the independently audited proof that a finite real set
$A$ satisfying $\FS(A)\subseteq4A$ has at most 97 elements.
A monotone change of the two mass variables replaces every positive
low-hole parameter by the endpoint 14. A coarse sumset estimate then
handles 1,313 of 1,374 structural roots directly. Only four roots need
the large-loss split; after refinement, only five of the 1,442 leaves
need nested envelopes. The finite proof shrinks from 45,928 to 18,280
integer models, from 3,750 to 1,455 interval checks, and from 209 to 94
integer certificates. This is a simplification of the proof of $N\le97$,
not a new numerical upper bound. The independently closed range remains
$71\le N\le97$; the stronger archived claim $N\le84$ still requires its
separate complete audit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and inherited structural reduction}
For a finite real set $A$, define
$\FS(A)=\{\sum_{x\in X}x:X\subseteq A\}$, including the empty sum.
Repeated summands are allowed in $4A=A+A+A+A$.
The previous report \cite{previous} excludes size 98 using the audited
upper bound 98 and structural lemmas from the preceding chain.
Here we retain its lattice proof, scalar hole bounds, covering units,
and actual-set-to-model derivation, but replace the final linear proof.
No previous endpoint certificate file is used by the new checker.

For a hypothetical size-98 set, the inherited reduction gives an integral
90-element set $D$, a core $E=P\cup\{0\}\cup(-Q)$ of size 82, and
\[
 a=|P|,\quad b=|Q|=81-a,\quad I=(a-40)(a-41),\quad
 \Omega=395-I,\quad T_r=r(r+1)/2.
\]
Put $R_p=\max P$, $R_q=\max Q$, $M_p=\max D$, $M_q=-\min D$,
and let $S_p,S_q$ be the positive and negative-magnitude masses of $D$.
The support of $E$ has $z$ holes; the support of $D$ has $w\le4z$ holes.
The covering slack is
$c=2|2D|+18\cdot90+85-|\FS(D)|$.
If $j$ covering units are empty, set $\delta=c-90j$.
For a retained center $v$, let $l_v,u_v$ be its lower and upper hull
losses and $\lambda_v=l_v+u_v$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss}
 \sum_{v\text{ retained}}\lambda_v\le\delta,\qquad0\le\lambda_v\le89.
\end{equation}
The common-lattice proof in \cite{previous} is essential: its refined
inner comparisons are at most $-388$ for $z=0$ and $-121$ for $z>0$,
whereas the discarded coarse comparison can equal 7.
We do not replace that argument by a finite solver result.

At low holes, the inherited scalar bounds give
\begin{align*}
 z=0 &: c\le227-I,\quad \varepsilon_p,\varepsilon_q\ge0,\\
 1\le z\le14 &: c\le174-I+2z,\quad
 \varepsilon_p\ge a-3+z,\quad\varepsilon_q\ge b-3+z,
\end{align*}
where $\varepsilon_p=\sum P-T_a$, and similarly on the negative side.
Thus $j\le2$. For high holes the unchanged exact scalar audit excludes
all-active states by a margin of at least 35. Only
$(a,z)=(40,56),(40,57),(41,56),(41,57)$ allow an empty unit.
They belong to H1, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:high}
 \delta\le1,\quad \varepsilon_p\ge240+9(a-40),\quad
 \varepsilon_q\ge249-9(a-40),\quad
 S_p+S_q+\delta-2K\le1842,
\end{equation}
where $K=|2D|$. There are 40 lattice orientations, 416 surviving low-hole
states, and 1,574 surviving high-hole states, exactly as before.

\section{Eliminate the hole variable by mass transport}
The preceding proof retained the same real variable $z$ in every
positive low-hole row. It is possible to eliminate it without replacing
correlated inequalities by unrelated worst-case bounds.

\begin{lemma}[Monotone endpoint transport]\label{lem:transport}
Every feasible positive low-hole model with $1\le z\le14$ maps to a
feasible model with $z=14$ by the change
\[
 t=14-z,\qquad \widehat S_p=S_p+t,\quad
 \widehat S_q=S_q+t,
\]
with all other coordinates unchanged. This holds for global, core,
and ordinary quantiles, and for every allowed large-loss refinement.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Here $t\ge0$, so nonnegativity is preserved. The rows involving $z$
or a mass variable have the following changes in their left sides:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
Row or expression&Change\\
\midrule
$\delta-2z$&$-2t$\\
$R_p+R_q+\delta-4z$&$-4t$\\
$2M_s+6R_s-2S_s+2z$&$0$\\
$\sum_{i=1}^4x_i^s-S_s+z$&$0$\\
$M_s+4R_s-S_s$, $\sum_{i=0}^4x_i^s-S_s$&$-t$\\
Upper quantile: $v-u_v-S_s$ plus unchanged terms&$-t$\\
$S_p+S_q+\delta-2K-4z$&$-2t$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
All other rows are unchanged, except the hole bounds: the new value
14 satisfies both. Thus no upper-bound row is violated. The argument
is uniform in $a$, the center choices, and the loss classification.
\end{proof}
The transported masses are auxiliary coordinates; they need not be
masses of another actual set. This is sufficient for a necessary linear
relaxation. The implication is from every actual low-hole state to the
endpoint model, not an assertion that the actual hole count equals 14.
In the rest of this report $S_p,S_q$ denote these transported coordinates
for positive low-hole models. Their mass bonuses are now
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bonuses}
 (\varepsilon_p,\varepsilon_q)\ge(a+11,b+11).
\end{equation}
The verifier checks the transport direction in every source row before
eliminating $z$. This algebraic check covers all real $t\ge0$; it is not
a sampling check at selected hole counts.

\section{A coarse proof spine with no inner-point variables}
The outer magnitudes are $p_1>p_2>p_3>p_4>M_p$ and their $q$ reflection.
The eight outer gaps are at least one when $j\le1$; for $j=2$ only weak
order is used. Always $M_s\ge R_s+4$ and $R_p\ge a$, $R_q\ge b$.
Use the two mass lower bounds on each side:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mass}
 S_p\ge T_a+\varepsilon_p+3R_p+6+M_p,
 \qquad S_p\ge T_{a-1}+4R_p+6+M_p.
\end{equation}
Their reflections hold with $p,a$ replaced by $q,b$.
The bonuses are zero for Z, \eqref{eq:bonuses} for P, and
\eqref{eq:high} for H1. As in the previous verifier, the first mass row
is multiplied by two and labelled H; the second is labelled Hhat.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}[Coarse double-sumset bound]\label{lem:coarse}
For the integral set $D$,
\[
 K\le M_p+M_q+R_p+R_q+21.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every pair except a pair of two positive outliers or two negative
outliers lies in $[-M_q-R_q,M_p+R_p]$. This integer interval contains
$M_p+M_q+R_p+R_q+1$ positions. There are at most ten unordered repeated
pairs among the four positive outliers and ten among the negative ones.
Adding these possible exceptions proves the bound.
\end{proof}
The cardinality identity and $w\le4z$ give
$S_p+S_q+\delta-2K\le1704-90j+4z$ at low holes.
Add twice Lemma~\ref{lem:coarse} to eliminate $K$ entirely:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spine}
 \boxed{S_p+S_q+\delta-2(M_p+M_q+R_p+R_q)\le B,}
\end{equation}
where $B=1746-90j$ for Z, $B=1802-90j$ for P, and $B=1884$ for H1.
The code forms this row by exact addition of the two source rows.

For every nonempty positive translate use the global quantiles
\begin{equation}\label{eq:global}
 -v-l_v-M_p\le-90,\qquad v-u_v-S_p-M_q\le-89,
\end{equation}
with reflected rows for negative centers. These are integer interval
counts and require no assumption that a center lies in its target hull.
For H1 ordinary quantiles are justified by $\delta\le1$.
Together with \eqref{eq:loss}, \eqref{eq:mass}, orders, and the inherited
branch/contributor rows, \eqref{eq:spine} defines the coarse model.
It has no $K$, no hole variable, no exposed inner points, and no
minimum-linearization variables. We also omit the separate loss cap,
joint span, and individual slot caps from every final model. Their
scalar consequences still justify the ranges and refinements below.

\subsection{A readable integer-row contradiction}
The certificate mechanism can be seen without an optimizer. Consider
Z0, branch A, with pair 2's positive contributor chosen. Write D$s i$
for the outer order row, G$s$ for $-R_s\le-r$, H$s$ for the doubled
first row in \eqref{eq:mass}, L$v$ for a lower quantile,
C for \eqref{eq:spine}, and L for \eqref{eq:loss}.
Take the following nonnegative row combination:
\begin{align*}
 &2(\mathrm{Dp3}+\mathrm{Dq3})+6(\mathrm{Dp4}+\mathrm{Dq4})
 +2(\mathrm{Gp}+\mathrm{Gq})+\mathrm{Hp}+\mathrm{Hq}\\
 &\qquad+2(\mathrm{Lp4}+\mathrm{Lp9}+\mathrm{Lq5}+\mathrm{Lq8})
 +2\mathrm C+2\mathrm L.
\end{align*}
Every resulting variable coefficient is nonnegative. The remaining
outer coefficients are $2p_3+2q_3$; all other surviving terms are
nonnegative loss coordinates. The right side is exactly
\[
 -2a^2+162a-4072=-792-2I<0.
\]
Thus this entire family is impossible. The other stored vectors are
checked in exactly the same way, with their quadratic right sides
maximized over their full integer parameter intervals.

\section{Refine only when the coarse spine needs it}
\subsection{Root coverage and center conventions}
Set $\lambda_p=p_1-p_2-p_3-p_4$,
$\alpha_p=p_2-p_3-p_4$, and $\beta_p=2p_2-p_1-p_3-p_4$.
The positive center list is
\[
 v^p=(\lambda_p+p_1,\ldots,\lambda_p+p_4,
 \lambda_p-q_1,\ldots,\lambda_p-q_4,\alpha_p,\beta_p),
\]
with the reflected list $v^q$. Labels \texttt{p0},\ldots,\texttt{q9}
mean these centers, not the outer magnitude variables.
Pair 1 is \texttt{p4,q4}. Branch A has $\beta_p+\beta_q\le0$ and
pair 2 is \texttt{p9,q9}; branch B has $\beta_p+\beta_q>0$ and pair 2
is \texttt{p5,q5}. The other sixteen units are singles.
The mode construction is the unchanged exhaustive construction in
\cite{previous}: enumerate the missing units, reflect a present pair 1
to its positive contributor, and enumerate pair 2's contributor.
If branch A has a missing single \texttt{p5} or \texttt{q5}, a present
pair 2 may split. Drop its individual loss rows, enumerate an actually
contributing member $s$, and retain $-\beta_s-M_s\le-1$.
This necessary row is not treated as a sufficient condition for contribution.
There are respectively 4, 70, and 578 modes when $j=0,1,2$.

\subsection{Two loss rules, with no blanket large-center enumeration}
A retained translate has $90-\lambda_v$ hull points. If this number
exceeds four, the core quantiles are valid:
\[
 -v-l_v-R_p\le-86,\qquad v-u_v-S_p-R_q\le-85.
\]
The derivation is the core interval count in \cite{previous}: an
outside translate using more than four hull points must use a core
point, since only four points on that side lie outside the core.

When $j=2$, the actual residual budgets are at most 47 in Z and 22 in P.
Thus all centers use core quantiles. In P2 the stronger ordinary rows
are valid because $\delta<\min(a,b)+5$, the minimum outside loss:
\[
 -v-l_v\le-b-5,\qquad v-u_v-S_p\le-a-4.
\]
This justifies the two-empty refinement, independently of whether the
final linear model retains its loss-cap row.

When $j=1$, the budgets are at most 137 in Z and 112 in P, both below
$2\cdot86$. At most one retained center has $\lambda_v\ge86$.
The complete refinement is therefore: either every center uses core
quantiles, or choose each retained center once as the unique large-loss
center, impose $\lambda_v\ge86$ there and use its global quantiles,
and use core quantiles elsewhere. The large-center ranges are
$a=34,\ldots,47$ in Z and $a=36,\ldots,45$ in P, as independently
checked from the original scalar budgets. Deleting slot caps weakens
these necessary models and causes no loss of coverage.

Only four roots actually require this split:
Z1 and P1 in branch A with pair 2 missing, or with single \texttt{p0}
missing and pair 2's negative contributor chosen. Each has 17 retained
centers and produces 18 leaves. All 136 other one-empty low-hole roots
are excluded directly by the global coarse model. In particular, the
large-loss location is never enumerated for those roots.

\subsection{The five leaves that need nested envelopes}
In the delivered proof, only these leaves use more than the coarse sumset bound:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{llllr}
\toprule
Family&Missing units&Pair-2 choice/member&Large center&$a$ range\\
\midrule
Z1&A: \texttt{p0}&negative contributor&\texttt{p8}&34--47\\
P1&A: \texttt{p0}&negative contributor&\texttt{p8}&36--45\\
Z2&A: \texttt{p0,p8}&negative contributor&none&34--47\\
Z2&A: \texttt{p0,q5}&negative actual member&none&34--47\\
P2&A: \texttt{p0,p8}&negative contributor&none&36--45\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Expose $x_0=R_p<x_1<x_2<x_3<x_4=M_p$ and their negative analogues
$y_i$, with integer gaps at least one. Use the two exposed mass rows
\[
 S_p\ge T_a+\varepsilon_p+\sum_{i=1}^4x_i,
 \qquad S_p\ge T_{a-1}+\sum_{i=0}^4x_i,
\]
and their reflections. The two coarse mass rows follow from these
and the first three gaps, so they need not be included again.
The four gaps also imply $M_p\ge R_p+4$.
For $t=1,2,3$ put $W_t=x_t+y_t$ and $s_t=2(4-t)$, and use
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nested}
 K\le2W_t+1+\binom{s_t+1}{2}
 +\sum_{i=t+1}^4\left[
 \min(W_t+1,x_i-x_{i-1})+\min(W_t+1,y_i-y_{i-1})\right].
\end{equation}
To prove this, replace the inner points through level $t$ by their
integer hull. Its double sum has $2W_t+1$ points. Each successive
external translate extends the union by at most the smaller of its
gap and $W_t+1$. At most $\binom{s_t+1}{2}$ pairs among the remaining
external points remain. Introduce one nonnegative variable for each
minimum, bounded above by both arguments. Actual sets choose the minima;
equality is unnecessary in the relaxation.
These leaves retain $K$, the coarse bound, and the original cardinality
row. They use only three envelope levels; neither level 0 nor level 4
is needed. They introduce 19 additional coordinates: six inner points,
$K$, and twelve minimum variables. No such coordinates occur in the
other 1,437 leaves.

\section{Exact verification and result}
Every final model is $Ax\le b(a)$, $x\ge0$, with integer $A$ and a
rational quadratic right side. A stored nonnegative integer vector $u$
is accepted only if $uA\ge0$ and
$\max_{a\in[L,U]\cap\mathbb Z}ub(a)<0$.
The maximum is computed at endpoints and, for a concave quadratic, the
floor and ceiling of the vertex when in range. The polynomial is built
symbolically; it is not fitted to sampled values.

The previous model imposed nested coordinates at every leaf; the table
does not claim that every previous certificate used them.
The new routing has 1,313 direct coarse roots, 54 two-empty coarse
refinements, three two-empty nested refinements, and four loss splits.
The splits have 70 coarse leaves and two nested leaves. The totals
include every branch, not just successful representative instances.
Model count falls by about 60.2\%, interval checks by 61.2\%, and stored
vectors by 55.0\%. These are proof-size comparisons, not timing claims.

The standard-library checker rejects a missing root, a missing actual
contributor, a missing large-loss outcome, interval gaps or overlaps,
invalid refinements, unknown rows, negative weights, and noncontradictory
weights. It checks transport before eliminating the hole coordinate.
The \texttt{--exhaustive} run additionally rebuilds every one of the
18,280 numerical models, compares every row with the symbolic formula,
checks every row combination, and compares the minimum pointwise gap
with the interval maximum. This run agrees with the interval result.
Standard and \texttt{-O} result files are byte-identical; fresh certificate
discovery reproduces the certificate file byte-for-byte; the previous
upper-97 checker reproduces its saved result unchanged. Twelve negative
controls pass. Actual commands, elapsed times, hashes, and comparisons
are recorded in \texttt{results/2026-10-16/proof\_method/}.

\Needspace{13\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}
Assuming the audited foundation and structural reduction stated in
\cite{previous}, every finite real set $A$ with $\FS(A)\subseteq4A$
satisfies $|A|\le97$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The foundation excludes sizes above 98. A hypothetical size-98 set
satisfies the unchanged scalar reduction. High-hole active cases are
excluded there; the remaining high cases map to H1. Every low-hole case
maps to one of the seven root families. Lemma~\ref{lem:transport} sends
positive low holes to their endpoint models. A directly certified root
is impossible. Every other root undergoes a valid exhaustive refinement
by the two loss rules. The resulting necessary models, including all
five nested exceptions, have strict exact integer-row contradictions.
Therefore no size-98 set exists.
\end{proof}
This proof does not establish $N\le96$, and its verifier explicitly
rejects a request to certify the next cardinality. The next research
step is to apply the simplified coarse spine and isolate its genuinely
necessary exceptions at size 97.
